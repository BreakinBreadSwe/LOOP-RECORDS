# Loop Records

Catalog app for Loop Records, the Stockholm techno and experimental house label (1993 to 1998), and its sublabel Plumphouse Records. Owner: Gordon Cyrus (luvlab.io).

## Working with Gordon

Gordon is blind and uses a screen reader, mostly on an Android phone. Everything built here must work fully with a screen reader: real headings, labelled controls, live-region announcements for status changes, focus management on navigation, no information conveyed by colour or position alone. Keep replies to him in plain, warm prose. No em dashes, no heavy bullet formatting.

The back office (staff only) may: Gordon asked for a push to breakinbread.com there, so it has a "Breakin Bread" section with "Send everything to Breakin Bread" and a dry run, plus a Breakin Bread order channel. The back office (never the public site) ends with "App developed by Breakin Bread · breakinbread.com" (linked).
* Respect every credit: every person on every release and track is searchable and links to their own page.
* Pasted artwork keeps its own shape. Never crop a sticker or insert in display; use `object-fit: contain`.
* Loop's colours, sampled from the 1993 flyer 7-inch: orange `#FF4400`, royal blue `#1F16B2`, cream. Orange is the accent, blue marks focus.
* Mobile first. Gordon tests on his phone.

## What is in this repository

* `src/app.template.html` is the shared page (public catalog plus back office shell). `src/admin.js` is the back office, spliced in at `/*__ADMIN__*/`. `src/runtime.js` connects the page to Supabase (staff sign-in by email link, live document store, artwork uploads, downloads) by implementing the small `window.claude` API the page was written against.
* `public/brand/` holds the real Loop logo, vectorized from the sleeves with potrace: `loop-spiral.svg` (from the LOOP 012 label) and `loop-wordmark.svg` (the italic LOOP RECORDS lettering from the LOOP 001 sleeve), both `fill="currentColor"`, plus PNGs in black, white and orange at 1600px and `loop-favicon.svg`. The header shows them through CSS masks (spiral in Loop orange, lettering in the text colour); the build swaps `__LOGO_SPIRAL__`/`__LOGO_WORD__` for file paths, or for data URIs in the Claude build.
* Look and feel (Back office, Look and feel; stored in `app_docs` collection `site`, document `look`, readable by everyone via migration 0006): optional light and dark background colours (with a contrast check), background tint from the current release, spiral and lettering colour ("changes with the releases' colours", a fixed colour, or Loop orange / text colour), screensaver on/off and delay (default on, 9 seconds), and custom CSS. Release colours come from `data/cover-colors.json` (strongest colour of each main cover, `scripts/cover_colors.py`), merged as `r.color`; the logo and the tab icon take the release's colour on its page and another catalog colour on each other page change; lettering falls back to the text colour below 3:1 contrast.
* Screensaver: a full-screen mosaic of the sleeves (whole, not cropped) that swaps two or three sleeves a second with mixed effects (fade, flip, swivel, page flip, slide; no zoom, no flat spin) on one continuous S-curve per move (cubic ease in and out, flips on a single timeline), each new sleeve decoded before it moves, now and then turning a whole row or column in a wave, never two of the same sleeve on screen (tiles skip what is showing and what is on its way in); `aria-hidden`, never starts during audio, video, typing or an open dialog, and any touch, key, scroll or focus change ends it; the touch that wakes it never activates what is underneath.
* Header: logo with STOCKHOLM 1993–1998 as a line under the lettering at the same width (an SVG with `textLength`), then monochrome icon buttons with `aria-label` and `title` (Releases, People, About, Back office, My page, theme, sign in/out). Theme is one button cycling Match device, Light, Dark. On phones (640px and narrower) the logo gets its own row at full width (up to 420px) with the era line under it, and search plus the icons share the row below; the header stays at the top, slides away while scrolling down and returns on scrolling up, at the top, on focus or on page change. From 900px search sits between logo and icons.
* Home: one row with the label chips (All, Loop, Plumphouse, Licensed) on the left and, right-aligned, ordering (Cat. no., A–Z by artist, Year by release date; tapping the active one again turns the direction around, undated always last) and the Mosaic icon; it wraps to two rows only on phones. Ordering is remembered (`loop-sort`) and also applies to search results and the People page (A–Z or first year). Searches add a Show row: Everything, Releases, People.
* Videos, like YouTube (Gordon: don't reinvent the wheel). `#videos` is a browse page: label filter chips and ordering on top, then one continuous grid of thumbnails (sleeve in the corner), each a link to `#video-<YouTube id>`. That opens the watch page: the player big on the left with title underneath and a meta row (release card with label name, artist, catalog number and year; a Share button using the phone's share sheet or copying the link), and a scrolling "Up next" column on the right (sticky, the next 20 in the current filter and order) with an Autoplay switch (remembered, on by default) that goes to the next video when one ends. Moving to another video keeps the same player and switches in place, so the back button walks through watched videos. The footer player's previous/next follow the same list. Likes and comments are not built yet (they need sign-in and a moderation decision). Page titles on Videos and People are smaller (`.pagetitle`). Header and body use the full window width (`.wrap` has no max width).
* Videos (`#videos`, header icon; `#video-<YouTube id>` links to one): "Loop TV". A stage with the player (YouTube IFrame API, plain embed if it can't load) and the release's sleeve, title and link beside it, tinted with the release colour; the logo takes that colour while it plays. "Play all, one after another" auto-advances; Previous and Next. Below, every unique video (237) as a thumbnail with its sleeve in the corner, grouped by release (catalog order) or by year, flat for A to Z, with the label filters and ordering.
* Clean YouTube player (`cleanPlayer` in the template): used on release, person and Videos pages. No YouTube controls, title bar or suggestions: `controls=0`, the iframe is 90px taller than its box at top and bottom so YouTube's bars fall outside, a transparent shield takes taps (tap = play/pause), the thumbnail covers the pause and end screens, and our own controls sit underneath like YouTube or Vimeo, in our own look: a thin full-width timeline right under the picture (orange progress, `--p`), then one row with play/pause and time on the left, mute and full screen (where supported) on the right; the slider speaks its time. The iframe is `aria-hidden` and out of the tab order; screen readers use our controls. Players are prepared ahead (cued, `autoplay:false`): the Loop TV stage as soon as the page opens, release and person page videos as they scroll within 300px (IntersectionObserver), so a single tap plays at once. If a phone still blocks playback, after 4 s with the video unstarted the shield lets one tap through and says "Tap the video once to start it". Only one video plays at a time. Gordon will design the info area under the player later.
* Mosaic view: the slideshow transition is an icon button cycling Random, Fade, Flip sideways, Flip up and down, Page turn; the release and video counts are never shown (Gordon: numbers feel wrong), only read out by screen readers; on phones the transition and mosaic buttons sit at the end of the label row (on screens under 390px at the end of the ordering row).
* Discogs profile and Search YouTube on person pages are staff only (`.staffonly`, shown by `revealStaff()` in admin.js).
* `npm run build` (`scripts/build.mjs`) writes `public/index.html`, `public/runtime.js`, `public/catalog.json`. Never edit those by hand. `public/art/` holds the pasted cover images.
* `data/catalog.json` is the full catalog: 62 releases, 156 people, tracklists, credits, video links. Shape `loop-records.catalog/v0`.
* `api/config.js` hands the browser the public Supabase URL and anon key. `api/breakin-bread/push.js` is the switched-off partner sync. `lib/bundle.js` builds the sync payload; `lib/server.js` holds server helpers.
* `supabase/migrations/0001_loop_backoffice.sql` is the relational schema (labels, people, releases, tracks, credits, splits, products, customers, orders, events, sync deals, staff, with row level security; money in öre). `0003_audio.sql` adds the public `audio` bucket for track sound files (staff upload, everyone plays, 500 MB per file allowed by the bucket; the Supabase plan's own upload limit also applies). `0002_app_docs.sql` adds the document store the back office uses today (`app_docs`), the public `artwork` storage bucket and sync-log columns. `supabase/seed.sql` loads the whole catalog, idempotent.
* Videos are linked to tracks: `trackForVideo` matches a track title (brackets removed, at least 3 letters, whole words, longest wins) inside the video title; 215 of 237 videos match. The footer and the player window name the track (position and title) with the release's square sleeve.
* The footer player shows from the start on every page (idle with a random linked track ready; Play opens the player window and starts it).
* Player window (`#pwin`, `pwin` in the template): slides up over the page above the footer when you tap the sleeve or title in the footer. Video big with our controls, the track title, release card (label, artist, catalog number, year, track position), the track's credits, the release's tracklist (tracks with a video are buttons that play it; the playing one is marked) and an Up next column. Escape or the close button closes it and pauses its video (YouTube may not play hidden); Play in the footer opens it again. Opening it while a video plays on a page carries that video on in the window from the same moment.
* Level meter (`#vu`): two thin vertical bars on the far right, between header and footer, while a sound file plays; peak markers; mono files show the same on both sides. It measures the real sound through Web Audio (the audio element uses `crossOrigin="anonymous"`; Supabase storage sends CORS headers). It never shows for YouTube videos, whose sound a page can't measure, and never fakes movement.
* Release pages: a track without a sound file but with a linked video gets "Play video", which opens the player window with that video playing (visible, so it stays within YouTube's rules) and drives the footer. Audio-only playback of YouTube (hidden player or ripped sound) is not done: Gordon asked twice; YouTube's terms forbid it.
* Footer player (`#dock`, `dock` in the template): a bar fixed at the bottom, hidden until something plays. Track sound files play through it (a Play button on each track with sound, its own audio element so playback carries on across pages, previous/next within the release, waveform timeline drawn from the file with Web Audio, falling back to a plain line if the file can't be read). While a video plays it works as a remote for that video (play/pause, previous/next in Loop TV, timeline, mute) and disappears when the video leaves the page. Never audio and video at once. YouTube audio is never ripped (YouTube terms and the uploaders' copyright).
* Track sound files: staff see "Add sound file for <position> <title>" under each track on a release page; visitors get a Play button that plays the full track in the footer player (Gordon chose full tracks, not previews). One `app_docs` document per track in collection `audio`, id `<release id>_<position>`. The partner bundle carries them as `audioUrl` on tracks and works.
* `tests/` runs with `npm test` (node --test). Run `npm test && npm run build` before committing.
* `data/catalog.json` is built from `data/discogs/` (raw Discogs release data) and `data/youtube.json` (extra videos found by search) by `scripts/build_catalog.py`. It carries no images; `data/artwork.json` maps release ids to cover images in `public/art/` (233 images, all 62 releases): `<id>-<n>.jpg` are the 59 images Gordon pasted for 18 releases in the Claude-hosted version (copied from its store on 7 Nov 2026; these always win), `<id>-discogs-<n>.jpg` are every Discogs image (front, back, labels, inserts) for the other 44 (the six "related" releases included, via `--include-related`), fetched by `scripts/fetch_discogs_art.py` at Discogs's limit of 25 a minute, and `lib/catalog.js` merges the two for the website and the partner sync. `data/artwork-db/` and `data/artwork-manifest.csv` record what was pasted in the Claude-hosted version.
* Python build scripts (Python 3, no dependencies): `build_catalog.py` rebuilds `data/catalog.json`, `make_seed.py` regenerates `supabase/seed.sql`, `build_claude_app.py` rebuilds the Claude-hosted version into `dist/` (raw catalog, no pasted covers, since that version keeps its own artwork store), `fetch_discogs_art.py` fetches missing Loop and Plumphouse covers from Discogs one at a time and lists them in `data/artwork.json`. The old `build_site.py` is replaced by `npm run build`.
